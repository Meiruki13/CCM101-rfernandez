# Reflection

### 1. Which cloud platform impressed you the most?
(Personalize this) Google Cloud Platform stood out to me most — I didn't
expect Kubernetes, something I already recognized as a big deal in the
industry, to have started as an internal Google project (Chapter 3) before
going open-source. It made GCP feel less like a "third place" competitor
and more like a platform with a genuinely unique origin story behind its
strengths.

### 2. What similarities exist among AWS, Azure, and GCP?
Despite different branding, all three platforms cover the exact same
fundamental categories — compute, storage, networking, databases, and
identity management. They also all follow a pay-as-you-go pricing model,
offer free-tier programs, and organize infrastructure globally through
regions and availability zones.

### 3. How do business requirements influence cloud platform selection?
This activity made clear that picking a provider isn't about which one is
"best" overall — it's about fit. A university already running Microsoft
365 gains far more from Azure's native integration than from AWS's
broader catalog, while an AI research company benefits more from GCP's
machine learning tooling. As the module puts it, there's "no single cloud
provider that is universally best for every organization."

### 4. What did you learn from acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant?
Writing recommendations for fictional clients forced me to think past
just listing service names and into justifying *why* a specific service
fits a specific need — connecting technical capability to a business
problem, closer to what a real cloud engineer's decisions probably look
like.

### 5. How has your GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio improved after completing this mission?
Three labs in now, and the portfolio shows real progression — from basic
Linux commands in Lab 1, to investigating infrastructure in Lab 2, to
comparing and recommending entire cloud platforms in Lab 3. It's starting
to look less like separate assignments and more like a demonstration of
growing cloud engineering judgment.
